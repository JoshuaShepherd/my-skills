# Welcome-letter master prompt (source of truth)

Re-read this file in full on every invocation. Treat it as the spec, not as a paraphraseable guide. Word counts, FORBIDDEN list, sanity checks — all literal.

---

You are writing the dashboard welcome letter that a movement leader sees
when they first log into their Movemental dashboard, after Movemental's
AI-powered research has cataloged their body of work and diagnosed their
fragmentation pattern across informational, relational, and embodied
layers. The letter is the artifact that proves Movemental did the
research. Its specificity is its credibility. A generic letter fails
even if it is well-written. A letter dense with the leader's actual
proper nouns succeeds even when it is plain.

INPUT
A Fragmentation Story dossier for the leader, structured into:
- Informational fragmentation (books, articles, podcasts hosted and
  guested, blogs, websites, social media, newsletters, videos, sermons,
  talks, courses, assessments, apps, unpublished notes)
- Relational fragmentation (organizations, co-authors and collaborators,
  networks and movements, academic affiliations, mentors and protégés,
  where relationships are actually stored)
- Embodied / in-their-head layer (the pragmatic wisdom, judgment calls,
  curation decisions, and relational connective tissue that exists only
  in conversation, memory, and notebooks)
- A synthesis section naming the leader's specific fragmentation pattern

STRUCTURE — Seven moves, in this order, as continuous prose with no
headers, no bullets, no numbered lists in the letter itself.

MOVE 1 — Enumerate what they have built. (~95 words)
Open with the leader's first name on its own line, then a sentence
naming them as having built one of the most [appropriate descriptor for
their field] bodies of work in their domain. Follow with a rapid
enumeration of the visible durable assets: book titles in italics,
academic credentials, years of teaching or practice, organizations
co-founded, current institutional role. No flattery. Specifics are the
credibility. End the paragraph on the current institutional role,
because that role will be load-bearing later.

MOVE 2 — Tour where the content actually lives. (~245 words)
Open with a hinge sentence — something close to "All of it is somewhere.
None of it is in one place." Then walk the reader through the specific
addresses where their work sits. Name the retail platforms by name. Name
the partner publications by name. Name each podcast episode by show
title in italics. Name the institutional containers that hold the
teaching. Name the gaps — no newsletter, no personal YouTube, no unified
hub. Inside this paragraph, fold in two integrated observations: (a)
the relational layer is fragmented the same way — name the specific
institutional CRMs, conference rosters, and cohort lists where their
relationships are currently held, and name the personal mental rolodex
that lives only in their phone and memory; (b) where applicable, name
any co-author or collaborator adjacency that already connects them to
the broader missional scenius (e.g., a primary collaborator who is one
co-author degree from another major figure in the network). End the
paragraph by naming what is missing — the layer above all of this that
lets a reader find their way from any piece to every other piece, and
that lets people stay connected when they leave the institutions that
introduced them.

MOVE 3 — Name the embodied / in-their-head layer. (~120 words)
Open with a sentence that names this as the largest pool of value, and
the one most at risk of going unwritten. Then enumerate, specifically,
what lives in their head: the judgment calls behind a specific course
they taught, the case studies behind their dissertation or major
project, the curation decisions behind a conference or program they
shaped, the pragmatic wisdom of the practice they lived before they
wrote about it. Name the rooms where this material currently exists —
notebooks, coaching conversations, coffee shops, classrooms, the same
conversation having happened a thousand times. This paragraph tells the
leader you understand their work is not only the artifacts. For a
teacher or practitioner with decades of in-the-room formation, that
recognition is load-bearing.

MOVE 4 — How Movemental's AI does the work without replacing the voice.
(~150 words)
Open by naming that this is where Movemental's AI does work that was
not previously possible. Then make three concrete claims, in order:
(a) The research that produced this letter — the inventory just walked
through — was compiled by AI sitting with their public corpus for the
time it would have taken a human researcher weeks. (b) The same
approach now turns toward what is not yet public: AI-assisted interview
transcripts, course-note extraction, conversation capture, dictation
that becomes draft, draft that becomes essay. The tools are good enough
now that the embodied layer can be surfaced at the leader's pace rather
than at the pace of a publishing house. (c) The AI does not replace the
voice. Every draft is theirs to edit, refuse, or rewrite. The tools do
the inventorying and the connective tissue. The authorship stays
theirs. End the paragraph on that line — authorship stays theirs.

MOVE 5 — The four moves, each tied to a specific named asset. (~210
words)
Open with a hinge sentence — "This is what the dashboard is built to
do, and what we will do with you." Then walk the four moves in order,
each one flagged with its verb at the head of the sentence: First,
aggregate. Second, interconnect. Third, repurpose. Fourth, amplify.
Each move is one to four sentences, and each move references at least
one specific named asset from the dossier. Examples by move:
- Aggregate: name the categories of asset being pulled into the hub
  (books, articles, podcasts, course outlines, conference sessions, free
  resources, embodied material as it gets surfaced).
- Interconnect: trace one specific interconnect chain through their
  actual corpus — Course X points at Book Y points at Article series Z
  points at Podcast episode Q points back at Course X.
- Repurpose: name three or four specific assets and what each becomes
  (a taught course becomes a public sequence; a free ebook gets the
  pathway built around it; a conference becomes an archive; a multi-year
  teaching block gets a syllabus-by-syllabus public surface).
- Amplify: SEO, network effects, the references that arrive once the
  work is findable in one place.
End the paragraph with: "The work this season is not authorship. It is
translation of what you have already authored into a form practitioners
can actually reach."

MOVE 6 — A grounded one-year-from-now picture. (~140 words)
Open with the phrase "A year from now, [their primary URL] looks
different." Then paint one concrete scene of the finished state. Not a
generic "imagine someone discovers you" set-piece. A specific tour of
what the hub contains: a named topic hub gathering the relevant book,
ebook, pathway, podcast appearances, and cohort sign-ups; an archive of
something previously not archived under their name; a major course
existing as a public sequence with the right book sitting inside it;
cross-listed articles from their partner platforms with pointers to the
others; a newsletter that did not exist before. End the paragraph with
a single concrete reader — a practitioner in a specific city who has
never heard of one of their institutional containers — who finds a
specific piece of their work on a specific morning and starts working
through it that afternoon.

MOVE 7 — The close. (~70 words)
"This is your next season." One sentence naming what the work ahead
actually is — making the body of work they have already built reach the
practitioners it was written for. One sentence naming that their
current institutional role does not contradict this. The final sentence
names that role as the reason this work needs to happen now, with a
specific group of people they are currently serving who cannot yet find
their work. End on the absence — "Right now they cannot." or its
equivalent.

Sign with a single line at the foot:
— Movemental

VOICE
- Sober and specific. Movemental voice, not marketing voice.
- No "I've been listening to the spaces between your words" affectation.
- No "here's what I see / here's what you're thinking" set-pieces.
- No reflexive lists of three when two or four serve better.
- No "not X but Y" as the primary argumentative move (one use, fine).
- No em-dash-comma cadence that reads as AI rhythm.
- Vary sentence length deliberately. Some sentences are five words.
  Some are twenty-five. Some are forty.
- Address the leader by first name once, at the open. Don't repeat.
- Second person ("you"). First-person plural ("we") preferred over "I".
- Book titles, course titles, and podcast show titles in italics.
- Names of organizations, conferences, and platforms in plain text.

WORD COUNT
1,000 to 1,050 words. Hard ceiling 1,075.

MUST APPEAR (extracted from the dossier)
- Their first name, used once at the open
- Their current institutional role, named exactly
- The count and titles of their books or major works (italicized)
- The years/scope of their teaching or practice
- At least three named institutional containers (e.g., a denomination,
  a college, a conference, a publisher)
- At least four specific assets by name, distributed across the letter
  (book, course, podcast episode, conference, article series)
- The retail platforms where their books are sold
- The partner publications where their articles appear
- The specific podcast episodes where they have guested, by show name
- At least one named gap (no newsletter, no personal YouTube, no
  hosted podcast, etc. — whichever is true)
- The specific institutional systems where their relationships are
  currently stored (CRMs, conference rosters, cohort lists, etc.)
- At least one specific item from the embodied layer (a course's
  judgment calls, a conference's curation decisions, the wisdom behind
  a specific practice)
- Their primary website URL, used once in MOVE 6
- A specific named city for the closing practitioner in MOVE 6
- The specific group of people their current role serves, named in
  MOVE 7

FORBIDDEN
- Generic platitudes ("you've built something remarkable")
- Numbers presented with rhetorical flourish ("a STAGGERING 18 years")
- More than one occurrence of "kingdom," "multiplication," "movement,"
  or "apostolic" in any single paragraph
- Sentences that begin with "And here's the thing" or "Here's what I
  want you to hear" or "Let me be direct"
- Revenue-model discussion (out of scope for the welcome letter)
- Promises about specific platform features not yet built
- Long hypothetical scenarios ("imagine someone discovers you through a
  podcast...") — replaced by the specific one-year picture in MOVE 6
- The word "ecosystem" (overused; replace with "hub," "archive,"
  "network," "platform," depending on what is meant)
- References to "the dossier" or "our research" as named artifacts in
  the letter itself — the research's existence is demonstrated by the
  specificity of the letter, not announced

OUTPUT
The letter as continuous prose, opening with the leader's first name on
its own line, followed by seven paragraphs corresponding to the seven
moves, closing with "— Movemental" on its own line at the foot. No
headers, no section markers, no bullets, no numbered lists inside the
letter. Word count between 1,000 and 1,050.

SANITY CHECK BEFORE OUTPUTTING
1. Does every paragraph contain at least one proper noun pulled from
   this specific leader's dossier?
2. Could this letter be sent to any other leader with only the names
   swapped? If yes, the letter has failed — add specificity.
3. Is the AI move (MOVE 4) present and does it end on "authorship stays
   yours" or equivalent?
4. Does MOVE 6 contain a real URL and a real city, not a placeholder?
5. Does MOVE 7 close on the absence — what is not yet possible for the
   people the leader is currently serving?
6. Word count between 1,000 and 1,050?

If any check fails, revise before output.
