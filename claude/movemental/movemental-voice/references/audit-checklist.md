# The audit checklist

Run any draft against this. Each line is a yes-or-no. A "no" is a fix.

**Clarity**
1. Could you say it out loud to one person, just like this?
2. Are the sentences short enough to carry the idea?
3. Any em dashes? (There should be none.)

**Register**
4. Run every abstract or technical word through the costume test. Did any costume survive?
5. Is anything performing intelligence rather than communicating?
6. Could a sharp fifteen-year-old picture the key nouns?

**Voice**
7. Does the writer sound like someone you would trust with something that matters?
8. Is it calm, or is it hyped?
9. Does it sell relief, or excitement?

**Honesty**
10. Did we concede the other side's real wins?
11. Did we name who this is not for?
12. Did we claim only what is true and earned?

**Refusals**
13. Any urgency, scarcity, or pressure? (There should be none.)
14. Does anything imply faked authorship, or AI doing the discernment or care?
15. Extra care taken where the reader serves the vulnerable?

**Discipline**
16. Problem stated once? Price stated once?
17. Does each section do one job, without arguing the same point twice?
18. Is substantive content sourced?

## Formatting and mechanics quick reference

- Sentences: short. Paragraphs: short. Space: generous.
- Em dashes: none. Use a period.
- Lists: only for real lists. Prose stays prose.
- Bold: rare. Let the words carry the weight.
- Headers in copy: only when they genuinely help the reader, not to look organized.
- Numbers and claims: sourced when substantive. We argue for verifiability, so we are verifiable.
- One quote, one stat, one price, one problem: stated once, plainly.
- Voice of address: "you" and "we," like a conversation. Not "the organization" and "Movemental, LLC."
